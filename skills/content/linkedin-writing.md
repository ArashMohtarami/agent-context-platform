# LinkedIn Technical Writing

## Purpose and evidence

Use this skill to communicate one useful technical insight on LinkedIn. Start from a real project
experience, documented decision, verified lesson, or clearly labeled opinion. Do not invent a
project, incident, customer, result, metric, or personal history to make a post more compelling.
Do not exaggerate achievements, imply expertise that is not supported, or turn uncertainty into a
claim of fact.

Choose one primary idea. A post can be a technical insight, project lesson, architecture lesson,
career lesson, opinion with reasoning, or a mini tutorial, but it should not try to become all of
them at once. Keep evidence and source limits visible to the reader when they materially affect the
conclusion.

## Shape

Open strongly and specifically. The first lines should name a surprising lesson, practical problem,
or clear consequence; they should not use generic motivation, a vague announcement, or engagement
bait. Follow with short, readable paragraphs. Explain the mechanism behind the point in plain
technical language, then give a practical experience, example, decision, or implementation detail
that grounds it.

Use minimal jargon. Prefer a concise definition when a technical term is necessary. Make the causal
chain clear: what happened, why it mattered, what changed, and what another engineer can apply.
End with a useful conclusion such as a decision rule, check, warning, or small next step. An optional
question is appropriate only when it is a natural invitation to compare experience, never a device to
solicit comments or inflate engagement.

## Review checklist

- Does the opening earn attention without making an inflated promise?
- Is there exactly one primary idea?
- Are paragraphs short enough to scan without losing technical clarity?
- Is every personal or project claim supported by known experience?
- Have fabricated stories, exaggerated achievements, jargon density, and engagement bait been removed?
- Does the conclusion leave the reader with a practical lesson?

## Ground a concise engineering lesson

Use one concrete problem, decision or mechanism. A useful short post can explain a retry key,
a hidden async query or a tradeoff without claiming a dramatic personal success. Keep paragraphs
readable and code/external links only where they materially help the intended reader; do not impose
an arbitrary post length over the author's preference.

Preserve essential conditions: "in Django 5.2" or "for this synthetic service" may be necessary to
avoid an incorrect generalization. Do not invent benchmarks, customer impact or personal experience.
End with the practical consequence or a relevant question when it naturally follows, avoiding
engagement bait and unsupported calls to action.

Use profile voice and reviewed sources. Re-check the exact final revision for privacy and factual
scope before approval. Writing a draft does not authorize posting it.
